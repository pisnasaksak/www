01 · Our story

Our name comes from the family table

In Manus culture, aunts and mothers hold a place of deep respect. When they have something important to share, they call the family together with one simple invitation: “Come kaikai liklik Pis na Saksak.” Come and eat a little fish and sago.

To sit and eat together is an invitation built on trust, care and community. It is not one you refuse, because someone who loves you has something to offer.

The Society carries that same gesture into the economy. It calls the people of Manus to the table and says: we have found a way to markets, to income and to opportunity. Come, let us show you the path.

“Like fish and sago: shared with care, eaten together.”

04 · What we export

Five export streams from Manus land and sea

Each product has its own regulator in Papua New Guinea. The Apex handles the licences, so a ward does not have to.

Copra and coconut

White copra and higher-value products such as virgin coconut oil, desiccated coconut and coconut cream.

RegulatorKokonas Indastri Koporesen (KIK)

Vanilla

Cured, dried and graded to international standards. PNG supplies about a tenth of the world’s vanilla.

RegulatorPNG Spice Industry Board · DAL

Cacao

Fermented and dried cocoa beans from ward gardens, graded at LLG centres.

RegulatorCocoa Board of PNG

Sea cucumber

Harvested within national management rules. This trade is reserved for citizen-owned businesses.

RegulatorNational Fisheries Authority (NFA)

Coastal finfish

Fresh reef and coastal fish handled under HACCP food-safety rules, which are compulsory.

RegulatorNational Fisheries Authority (NFA)

Two layers of licensing

Every export from Papua New Guinea needs two layers of approval. The foundation steps come first. Then each product gets its own licence from its regulator.

Copra focus: KIK is phasing out black copra export licences. Our focus is white copra and higher-value coconut products, where Manus has untapped export capacity.
  1. IPA registrationInvestment Promotion Authority. Confirms the Society as a citizen enterprise.
  2. Tax identification numberInternal Revenue Commission. Needed to receive export payments.
  3. Customs exporter registrationPNG Customs Service, with a licensed customs agent for each shipment.
  4. Quarantine clearanceNAQIA certificate for each shipment of copra, vanilla and cacao. Fish use NFA health certificates.
  5. Commodity licenceFrom KIK, the Spice Industry Board, the Cocoa Board or the NFA, depending on the product.

07 · Manus Unit Trust Fund

Wealth that stays in the family

The Manus Unit Trust Fund (UTF) helps members build savings that grow for their children and grandchildren.

Money comes from member contributions and a loyalty programme on fuel, wholesale and retail purchases.
The Society is the legal trustee and must act in the best interests of members.
Unit prices, values and payouts are worked out openly and reported to members.

08 · Governed in the open

Rules that protect every member

Our constitution (refined draft v2.0, now under legal review) sets clear rules for how the Society is run and how members’ money is kept safe.

One member, one vote

Every member has an equal voice at general meetings, whatever the size of their shareholding.

Protected foundations

The three-tier structure and the Unit Trust Fund are entrenched rules. They cannot be changed easily.

Power at the lowest level

Each Ward and LLG cooperative runs its own affairs. Decisions are made as close to the community as possible.

Conflicts declared

Directors and officers must declare personal interests. No investment may go to a business they secretly hold.

Audit and fair buying

Independent audit, risk oversight and clear procurement rules guard the Society’s assets.

Cooperative principles

We follow the seven principles of the International Co-operative Alliance, including concern for community.
